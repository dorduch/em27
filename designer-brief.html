EM27

A Championship Manager–style game where you play an Engineering Manager at a real tech company. Mobile first, played in short bursts. We need you to design how it plays and how it looks.

01 The game in one minute

You run a team of six engineers at Vercel, Stripe or Google. Every week you have 8 calendar slots and more demands than time: someone is burning out, a recruiter is poaching your best engineer, the skip-level wants "a quick deck", the CEO has an idea. You decide what gets your time, how you respond, and what you ignore. You staff your projects and pick who's on call, then tap Continue.

On Friday your plan is tested, CM match-day style: a short text play-by-play of projects shipping (or not), the on-call pager and the things you ignored coming back. Twelve weeks make a quarter, which ends in a quarterly review.

A full run is a career: climb from EM to Senior EM to Director (you win). Get fired once and you job-hunt at another company, a level down if your reputation is weak. Get fired twice and the run is over. Strong quarters bring poaching offers that tempt you to leave your squad. Every run ends with a shareable recap card.

Tone: satirical but grounded. The target reaction is "this literally happened to me." The audience is engineers and EMs, so insider jargon is welcome (on-call, PIP, skip-level, L5, RFC, "quick sync?").

Design stance: strategy first. Players should roughly predict what a choice does. Every choice shows its slot cost and hint tags, and every stat change can show its cause.

02 What we need from you

  1. Core loop & flows: plan the week → Continue → Friday → back page, plus the mid-quarter check-in, the quarterly review, and the career moments: promotion, getting fired → job hunt, poaching offer, end of run.
  2. Screen designs (mobile, ~390px): every screen in section 05, with key states (empty, overbooked calendar, must-respond item, ignored item, good and bad Friday).
  3. The calendar: the 8-slot budget is the core mechanic. It must be visible at all times while planning, and spending, refunding and overbooking slots should feel physical.
  4. A small UI kit: type, color, 1–20 attribute numbers, calendar slots, intent buttons with cost and hint tags, list rows, meters, stat-change arrows with causes, company badges.
  5. Feedback & feel: how a choice feels to commit to, how Friday builds tension line by line, and what makes the quarterly review dramatic.
  6. Gameplay suggestions: push back on anything in sections 03–04 that makes the game less fun. Balance numbers are ours, but the shape of the game is open.

03 Core loop

Week N ├─ MONDAY Inbox: 5–7 demands · Calendar: 8 slots ├─ PLAN Pick an intent per item (0–3 slots) or ignore it │ Book 1:1s · assign projects · set on-call ├─ CONTINUE Blocked only by the week's one must-respond item ├─ FRIDAY Play-by-play: projects, pager, ignored items └─ BACK PAGE Headline + stat changes, each with its cause Week 6 Mid-quarter check-in with your skip-level Week 12 Quarterly review → promotion · stay · fired Fired 1st time: job hunt at another company · 2nd time: run over Strong Poaching offer: accept · decline · use as leverage End Director (win) or 2nd firing → recap card

A week should take 3–5 minutes, and a quarter about 45. Players must be able to stop mid-week and come back later.

06 Look & feel

  • Direction: Championship Manager 01/02 meets a 2000s corporate intranet: dense, tabular, beveled panels, numbers everywhere. Then made genuinely usable on a phone.
  • Dense but readable: list rows with a few key numbers, then tap to drill into the full detail. No wide tables on mobile.
  • Touch: targets of at least 44px, nothing hover-only, playable one-handed.
  • Desktop: the same layout in a centered frame is fine for the MVP.
  • Light and dark themes are both expected.

07 Constraints

  • No company logos. We use real names only, shown as generic badges (initials and a color). Nothing should imitate a company's brand.
  • No real people. All characters are fictional.
  • All event text is generated, so titles, bodies and choice labels vary in length. Design for 1 line and for 4.
  • After Continue there is a wait of a few seconds while text is generated. The Friday commentary is designed to cover it; plan for lines appearing at a steady pace.
  • The game is installable to the home screen (PWA), so it needs an app icon and a splash screen.

08 Out of scope for the MVP

Accounts and sign-in (saves stay on the device in v1), leaderboards, relationships between engineers, typing your own replies, sound. Levels above Director.
